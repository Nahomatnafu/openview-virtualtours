# OpenView v04 content plan

## The direction

Warm and conversational, with a little wit. OpenView should sound like someone who understands how much effort it takes to arrange a visit, and has a useful way to make it easier.

The central idea is **giving people a look around without making everyone rearrange their day**. Time, distance, and freedom give the copy something real to talk about. The camera and editing explain how we deliver it.

This is a proposed copy direction for discussion. The full rewrite and new imagery have not been applied to the site. The navigation capitalization and missing-space fixes are prepared on v04.

## What changes in the writing

- Start with a recognizable situation: a family planning a move, a guest checking a rental, or a visitor deciding whether to make the trip.
- Use complete thoughts and varied sentence lengths. Replace repeated two-part slogans such as “One visit. Every angle.”
- Put the useful detail before the clever line. A little wit should come from the situation, not from adding jokes to every section.
- Keep the service easy to understand: we visit, photograph, edit, connect the views, and provide a hosted tour within a day.
- Give each section one job. Avoid repeating “your space,” “every angle,” and “one link” in every heading.
- Keep photographs part of the story. Photos catch attention; a tour helps someone understand how a place fits together. Calling photography outdated would undercut part of OpenView’s own service.
- Use the broader audience throughout the page. Apartments remain real examples of completed work, while vacation rentals, cafés, museums, and galleries are presented as spaces OpenView can serve.

## Proposed homepage flow

### 1. A clear invitation

Keep the existing hero headline, **“Let them look around.”** It already feels direct and fits the design. Give the supporting text more purpose:

> Give people a feel for the place before they make the trip. We create 360° tours they can explore whenever it suits them, with photography, editing, and a finished link delivered within a day.

Primary action: **Let’s talk about your space**

Secondary action: **Take a look around**

Replace the floating caption with **“We visit. Your tour is ready the next day.”** This is a proposed replacement; the current caption’s spacing is fixed separately.

Simplify the service strip to practical information: **360° photography · Editing included · Ready within a day · Hosting handled**. Let the story sections carry the personality.

### 2. Give the time-saving benefit a real situation

Suggested heading: **Before they book the flight.**

> Moving from another state is enough of a project already. A family shouldn’t need plane tickets just to find out the layout won’t work for them. Let them explore online first, then make the trip when there’s a place they’re excited to see.

Follow with the benefit for the person arranging viewings:

> And on your side, there’s less back-and-forth over calendars. Send a tour someone can open after work, revisit with their partner, or share with a roommate. You can still meet in person, with a lot of the first questions already answered.

Visual brief: one photographic airplane cutout beside this section, with a restrained travel-path accent if useful. Use a real, appropriately sourced image with no airline branding. Keep it away from body text on mobile. Avoid turning the page into a travel advertisement.

### 3. Broaden the kinds of spaces

Suggested heading: **What would you like to show people?**

- **Homes and apartments:** Let someone work out whether the layout suits them before arranging a viewing.
- **Vacation rentals:** A family booking from a few states away can get a better feel for where everyone will sleep, eat, and spend time together.
- **Cafés:** Show the seating and the atmosphere, so people can picture stopping in or settling down for an afternoon.
- **Museums and art galleries:** Give visitors a look through the galleries before they arrive, or a way to explore from home.

Visual brief: place an official Airbnb logo beside the vacation-rental example. Use it to identify the audience, without suggesting a partnership or promising that tours can be embedded in Airbnb listings. Keep the other audience labels simple instead of giving every item another decorative icon.

### 4. Explain the work without the slogans

Section heading: **Here’s how your tour comes together.**

Keep the four interactive tabs and existing photographs. The step labels already do the organizing, so each panel can use a short, natural explanation instead of another paired headline.

**Capture**

> We’ll arrange a time to visit and bring the Insta360 X5 and tripod. You get the space ready and let us in; we photograph the rooms and the details people will want to see.

**Edit & stitch**

> Back at the desk, we adjust the light and color in Lightroom and stitch the images together. The aim is a clear, natural view that feels like the place itself.

**Connect**

> We add the points people click to move between rooms. That way, they can follow the layout, double back, and spend a little longer wherever they’re curious.

**Host & share**

> We can handle the hosting and sharing for you. Already have a platform you like? We can work with Kuula, Matterport, or another option and agree on the setup before the shoot.

Keep the next-day delivery promise visible nearby, with the existing larger-project scheduling qualification in the FAQ.

### 5. Let the examples do their job

Suggested heading: **Go on, have a look.**

> Here are a few tours we created at U-Square. Open one, look around, and see how the rooms connect.

Keep the small selection of real tours. Avoid presenting apartments as evidence of work completed for cafés, museums, or galleries.

### 6. Finish with a straightforward conversation

Suggested closing heading: **Have a place in mind?**

> Tell us a little about it. We’ll talk through what you want to show, answer your questions, and find a day that works.

Button: **Book a call**

Secondary link: **Ask a question**

For the contact page, replace “Open up the possibilities” with **“Tell us about your place.”** Keep the calendar and preferred-date request clearly explained.

## The writing session

1. Review the hero and travel paragraph first. They establish how conversational and playful the rest of the site should be.
2. Read them aloud. Mark anything that sounds like an advertisement instead of something OpenView would actually say.
3. Carry that voice through the audience section, process, examples, and contact page. Keep pricing and FAQ answers especially direct.
4. Cut repeated ideas and decorative labels. The finished page should remain concise even with the broader audience.
5. Apply the agreed draft and the two new visual assets on v04. Check mobile wrapping, spaces around line breaks, buttons, image loading, and booking links before updating the preview.

## Keep intact

The forest-green and chartreuse design, eye mark, existing photography, selected tours, business contact details, platform logos, working calendar, and “Based in Minnesota and beyond” wording remain the foundation. Main and v03 stay unchanged during this content pass.
